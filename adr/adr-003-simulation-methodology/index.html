    ADR 003: how the simulation earns the right to a number¶

    Status: accepted Date: 2026-10-20

    Context¶

    Every figure this project publishes is a simulation. The live feed path in pob.feeds exists, has tests against recorded responses, and has never been run against the real feeds, because the environment it was built in cannot reach epss.cyentia.com or cisa.gov. That is stated in docs/metrics.json, in the README, and here, because a simulated number presented as a measured one is the single most damaging thing a project like this can do.

    A simulation of a backlog can produce any result its author wants by choosing parameters. The methodology below is the set of constraints that make the output worth reading, and each one was added because the undisciplined version of the measurement was wrong.

    Decision¶

    Sourced and chosen parameters are labelled separately¶

    CorpusSpec.describe() splits its parameters into three groups: sourced, chosen, and scale. Exactly one parameter is sourced, from FIRST's EPSS model documentation:

    0.0268 of CVSS 3.x CVEs score at or above EPSS 0.1. Everything else is a declared choice. Printing them in separate groups stops a reader from inferring that the whole distribution came from somewhere.

    The realized values are reported next to the targets, because a generator does not necessarily produce what it was asked for. The realized share at or above 0.1 came out at

    0.0235 against the sourced 0.0268, and the realized rank correlation at

    0.225 against a requested 0.25. Those are the numbers the comparison actually ran on.

    The parameter the conclusion depends on is swept, not argued for¶

    The correlation between severity and exploitation probability is unknown and decides the whole comparison: at a correlation of 1, ranking by severity and ranking by exploitation probability are the same ranking. Experiment B sweeps it from 0.0 to 1.0 and reports the verdict at each point. The conclusion holds across the range and the two orderings converge at the top of it, which is what should happen if the mechanism is the one claimed. A sweep that produced the same verdict at every point including correlation 1.0 would be evidence of a bug, not of robustness.

    Early on it did exactly that, because the verdict function called a gap of 0.003 a win. It now requires a margin of 0.02 before calling either side ahead, and reports a tie otherwise.

    Results are averaged over seeds, and the spread is published¶

    Twelve seeds, and the spread across them in the same table as the mean.

    This is the rule that cost the project its best headline. A single seed showed the severity ordering covering 0.085 of the known exploited findings against a shuffled backlog's 0.211, which reads as "ranking by CVSS is worse than ranking at random", and which is the most quotable sentence this benchmark could have produced. Over twelve seeds it is

    0.382 against

    0.211. The striking version was noise. Its spread across seeds is

    0.645, wide enough that single runs land almost anywhere, which is why the spread column exists and why twelve seeds is stated as a limitation rather than as sufficiency.

    Ceilings are capacity aware¶

    Window attainment is reported as a share of what is reachable, not of the backlog.

    3,248 findings are past their window on day one and no ordering can bring them back, and a programme with finite capacity cannot reach every remaining deadline either. The ceiling is computed by a greedy earliest deadline first pass over the findings whose deadlines are still reachable at the given capacity, which gives

    170 findings.

    The first version of this ignored capacity and reported a ceiling of 510, which understated every ordering's attainment by a factor of three. A denominator nobody can reach makes every ordering look equally bad, which conveniently flatters the comparison the project was making.

    Capacity holds its ratio to the backlog across scales¶

    The sweep and the headline run at the same capacity share of the backlog. They did not at first, and a shuffled baseline that should have matched the headline's

    0.211 came out at 0.54, because the sweep was giving the programme more capacity per finding. Two numbers in the same document that cannot both be right is the cheapest bug to find and the most expensive to publish.

    Consequences¶

    • The benchmark takes longer, because twelve seeds times six sweep points times eight orderings.
    • Every number in docs/results.md is generated from docs/metrics.json by tools/generate_results_doc.py, with --check in CI, so prose cannot drift from the measurement.
    • The limitations are a document rather than a sentence. See docs/limitations.md.
