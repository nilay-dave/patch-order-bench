    Results¶

    Generated by tools/generate_results_doc.py from docs/metrics.json. Reproduce with make bench.

    Every figure here is a simulation¶

    Every figure in this file is a simulation. The live feed path in pob.feeds has never been run against the real feeds, because the environment this was built in cannot reach epss.cyentia.com or cisa.gov. One distributional parameter is sourced, from FIRST's EPSS model documentation: 3,735 of 139,473 CVSS 3.x CVEs score at or above 0.1. Every other parameter is a declared choice, and the one everything depends on, the correlation between severity and exploitation probability, is swept in experiment B rather than argued for.

    The corpus¶

    5,000 findings over about 852 CVEs, 134 of them known exploited, averaged over 12 seeds. 3,248 findings were already past their PCI window on day one, which is what a real backlog looks like and is why window attainment has a ceiling.

    The realized rank correlation between severity and exploitation probability came out at 0.225, and the realized share of CVEs at or above EPSS 0.1 at 0.0235 against a sourced target of 0.0268. Both are reported because they are what the measurement ran on, rather than what was asked for.

    The comparison¶

    A programme doing 40 remediations a week for 26 weeks, which is 1,040 in total against a backlog of 5,000. Capacity has to bind, or there is nothing to prioritise.

    At this rate the most window compliance any ordering could achieve is 170 findings, and the attainment column is a share of that rather than of the backlog.

    ordering KEV coverage spread over seeds effort to 90% of KEV window attainment exposure removed
    cvss 0.382 0.645 never reached 0.132 0.347
    epss 1.000 0.000 274 0.075 0.939
    kev-then-epss 1.000 0.000 121 0.084 0.939
    kev-then-cvss 1.000 0.000 121 0.107 0.657
    exposure 0.991 0.056 384 0.059 0.949
    due-date 0.212 0.357 never reached 0.000 0.222
    window-first 0.202 0.446 never reached 0.649 0.172
    random 0.211 0.108 never reached 0.064 0.219

    No ordering wins on both axes, and that is the result. epss covers the known exploited findings best; window-first meets the most compliance windows; neither is second at the other's job. A programme has to choose which it is optimising, and most choose neither on purpose, because they rank by whatever the scanner printed.

    Ranking by severity covers 0.382 of the known exploited findings against a shuffled backlog's 0.211. It is better than no ordering, and it is a long way behind putting the catalogue first.

    How much to trust these¶

    The spread column is the range across 12 seeds, and for cvss it is 0.645, which is wider than most of the differences in the table. A single run can land almost anywhere inside that, which is why the benchmark averages and why a reader wanting a tighter figure should raise the seed count rather than quote one of these to three decimal places.

    Does the conclusion survive its own assumption¶

    The correlation between severity and exploitation probability is the parameter nothing in this project knows and everything depends on: at a correlation of 1 the two orderings are the same ordering. So it is swept rather than argued for.

    correlation realized severity ordering KEV first shuffle verdict
    0.00 -0.027 0.268 1.000 0.190 exploitation ordering wins
    0.20 0.162 0.368 1.000 0.190 exploitation ordering wins
    0.40 0.354 0.485 1.000 0.190 exploitation ordering wins
    0.60 0.555 0.741 1.000 0.190 exploitation ordering wins
    0.80 0.768 0.936 1.000 0.190 exploitation ordering wins
    1.00 1.000 0.997 1.000 0.190 exploitation ordering wins

    The conclusion holds across the range. It never flips, and at the top of the range the two converge, which is what should happen if the mechanism is the one claimed: at a perfect correlation the severity ordering and the exploitation ordering are the same ordering.

    Runtime¶

    findings generate slowest ordering seconds
    5,000 0.05s window-first 0.014s
    50,000 0.57s window-first 0.222s
    500,000 7.57s window-first 2.884s

    Ranking and scheduling 500,000 findings takes 2.9 seconds for the slowest ordering. A ranking a programme cannot rerun weekly is a ranking it will stop rerunning.
